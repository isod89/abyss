The sound lab

ABYSS picks each piece's sounds from its sound bank: for each world, a short list of sounds per layer (drone, pad, low, melody, bells, swell). Picking that bank is the biggest job left before ABYSS ships on the FM-1, and your ears can help.

  1. Pick a world below.
  2. Listen to each sound (▶) as its layer plays it, remove what doesn't fit (×), add what's missing (+ add a sound).
  3. Try it: go to Play and press ▶. ABYSS now picks from your bank (‹ › on a layer goes through its sounds).
  4. Send it to me with the button on the right. Your bank is saved in this browser, so you can come back to it.

ABYSS balances the sounds of its own bank automatically, community sounds included. A sound you add plays at its raw level for now: judge the sound, not how loud it is.

Send your bank

Sends your bank, your name and your notes straight to sodwavecontact@gmail.com. No email app, no attachment. You can send again after more changes.

    Where its rules come from
    
      
    ABYSS builds each piece toward one moment, at about two-thirds of its length: what research on musical chills (Sloboda; Grewe; Guhn; Bannister & Eerola) found gives people shivers. A long rise, a breath of near-silence just before, a new voice entering, one unexpected chord, an appoggiatura on the highest note, the register opening at both ends, the theme coming back.
    
      
    Cinematic: 56–72 BPM, the chord loops of modern-classical film music, strings and cello, a felt piano repeating one note across octaves, tape hiss, sometimes a clock. Cosmic: beatless drifts, a Berlin-school sequencer or a slow beat that arrives late; lydian and major, sus and add9 chords; wind; the "lift" (the key moves a third and a new voice enters); the pitch drifting a few cents like an old analog synth. Dark: no pulse; drones a semitone or a tritone apart, a sub that breathes, rumble, distant struck metal, one rise of minutes, rough clusters at the top, then the void and the decay. One dark piece in four is a tape loop played until it falls apart.
    
      
    It is the same brain as on the FM-1, compiled for the browser: a seed plays the same pieces here and on the machine.

    How to play
    
    
      
      
    1. ▶ Play. ABYSS composes endless ambient music: pieces of 6 to 15 minutes that flow into each other, each built toward one peak. Pick a world: cinematic, cosmic or dark.
      2. 
      
    2. The hands: LIGHT (shadow to light: filters, register, and the harmonies of the next pieces), SPACE (reverb and echo), DENSITY (how much it plays), TIME (slower to faster), TENSION (how rough the peak gets). Drag a knob up or down.
      3. 
      
    3. The gestures: ask for a RISE, the PEAK NOW, CALM, a BREATH, a LIFT, a new theme or harmony, HOLD this section, the VOID, the NEXT PIECE, or FADE out. ABYSS answers in its own time.
      4. 
      
    4. The layers: every layer has its volume, filter and space, ‹ › to change its sound, ↻ for another by chance, ◆ to keep it, M to mute.
      5. 
      
    5. Textures: SHIMMER (the reverb an octave up) and TAPE (hiss and wow).
      6. 
    
    
    
    Use headphones or good speakers: a lot happens in the lows. Give it a few minutes; the peak comes after a long rise.
    
    
    Want to help? The Sound lab tab lets you build your own sound bank and send it to me.
    
    
    Keyboard: space = play, F = show mode. Works best in Chrome or Edge on a computer.
